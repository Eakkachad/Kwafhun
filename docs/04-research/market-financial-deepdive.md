### 1.2 Thailand Tutoring / Exam-Prep Market (closest revenue analog to "willingness to pay for career/education guidance")
- Older baseline: **private tutoring in Thailand ~8 billion baht/year**, and separately **"tutoring school income totaled 10 billion baht from ~535,000 students" (14% of all students)** — data appears to be from **2013–2015** (SCBEIC / Bangkok Post era reporting, referenced in a 2015-dated Bangkok Post op-ed).
  Source: SCBEIC (Siam Commercial Bank Economic Intelligence Center), "Tutoring schools growth opportunities and the impact of tax collection," https://www.scbeic.com/en/detail/product/1260 — data ~2013–2015 — **[VERIFIED]** (fetched; explicitly states "Tutoring school income totaled 10 billion baht from approximately 535,000 students," and data year identified as 2013–2015) — Thai-specific.
- More recent (2024–2025): **KASIKORN Research Center — Thailand tutoring-school revenue forecast THB 3.3 billion in 2025, +9.2% YoY growth**; regional growth split: outside-Bangkok tutoring revenue growth projected **23.8%/yr (2021–2025)** vs Bangkok **4.2%/yr**.
  Source: KASIKORN Research Center, "International schools in Thailand are proliferating beyond Bangkok, a trend also evident in the tutoring sector," https://www.kasikornresearch.com/en/analysis/k-social-media/Pages/Inter-School-IAO120-FB-27-02-25.aspx — Feb 2025 — **[VERIFIED]** (fetched directly) — Thai-specific.
  - **CAUTION / internal inconsistency flagged**: the 3.3B baht (2025) KASIKORN figure is much smaller than the older 10B baht (2013-15) SCBEIC figure — these may measure different scopes (e.g., registered "โรงเรียนกวดวิชา" formal tutoring-school segment only, vs. broader private tutoring incl. informal/individual tutors). Do NOT average these; cite each with its own year and note the segment differs. Recommend using the newer KASIKORN number (3.3B, 2025) as the more defensible "registered tutoring schools" data point, and citing the older 10B figure only as historical context.
- Separate: **spending on "cram schools and special training" contracted 0.7% YoY to 1.49 billion baht in 2024** (household expenditure category, narrower than school revenue).
  Source: found via search snippet referencing Nation Thailand, "Tuition fees and related expenses of students at Thai schools higher in 2024," https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40039023 — 2024 — **[UNVERIFIED]** (snippet only, not fetched) — Thai-specific.
- **International schools in Thailand reached ~USD 2.5 billion in 2024** (different market, useful as reference for "premium education spend" ceiling, not tutoring per se).
  Source: Khaosod English, "International Schools in Thailand Thrive, Reaching $2.5 Billion in 2024," https://www.khaosodenglish.com/featured/2024/09/08/international-schools-in-thailand-thrive-reaching-2-5-billion-in-2024/ — 2024 — **[UNVERIFIED]** (snippet only) — Thai-specific.

### 1.3 กสศ. (EEF – Equitable Education Fund) Budget — key SAM/funding-mechanism anchor
- **EEF FY2567 (2024) Cabinet-approved spending plan: THB 7,985,786,100 (≈7.99 billion baht)**, across 9 programs. Largest line item: "Equity Guarantee System Development" (conditional cash transfers to poor students) at **5,359.05 million baht**. Other lines: Vocational & Higher Education Support 1,025.92M; Teacher Development 394.11M; Quality School Development 280M; Innovation & Research 267.5M; Out-of-System Youth Support 251M; Geographic Education Management 70M; Knowledge Communication 46.5M; Administration 291.71M.
  Source: EEF (กสศ.), "ครม. เห็นชอบแผนการใช้เงิน กสศ. ปีงบฯ 2567 วงเงิน 7.9 พันล้าน...," https://www.eef.or.th/news-23012023-budget/ — FY2567 (2024) — **[VERIFIED]** (fetched directly) — Thai-specific.
- **Student coverage under this plan: 2,636,304 students/instances** receiving subsidies from early-childhood through primary completion, targeting the poorest 15% of households nationally. Useful upper bound for "students in poverty needing intervention" — a plausible SAM anchor for the free/subsidized side of the คว้าฝัน user base.
  Same source as above — **[VERIFIED]** — Thai-specific.
- **Higher Education Vocational Innovation Fund (ทุนนวัตกรรมสายอาชีพชั้นสูง) FY2567: ~2,500 free scholarships/year** (tuition + monthly living stipend at partner vocational institutions).
  Source: EEF, "โครงการทุนนวัตกรรมสายอาชีพชั้นสูง ปี 2567," https://www.eef.or.th/notice/career-capital-1223/ — 2567 (2024) — **[UNVERIFIED]** (from search-snippet synthesis, not independently fetched) — Thai-specific.
- **ทุนเสมอภาค (Equity Fund) FY2567: 97.88% retention rate** among scholarship recipients, with reduced malnutrition trends reported. Useful as an outcome/impact stat, not a market-size number.
  Source: search-snippet reference to EEF FY2567 reporting — **[UNVERIFIED]** — Thai-specific.
- **2x tax deduction for donations to EEF extended through 31 December 2571 (2028)**, covering the period 1 Jan 2567 – 31 Dec 2571 (2024–2028). Government's own projected annual revenue cost of this incentive: **~50 million baht/year**. Applies to both individuals and businesses donating via the Revenue Department's e-Donation system.
  Source: EEF, "ครม. เคาะต่อเวลาอีก 5 ปี 'ลดหย่อนภาษี 2 เท่า บริจาคให้ กสศ.' ออกไปถึงสิ้นปี 2571," https://www.eef.or.th/news-271223/ — Dec 2023/FY2567 announcement — **[VERIFIED]** (fetched directly) — Thai-specific. **This is a strong, ready-to-cite funding-mechanism precedent** — corporates/individuals get 2x tax deduction for donating toward the same poor-student population คว้าฝัน targets.
  - Related general context: the 2x deduction previously applied broadly to donations to educational institutions but, as of tax year 2568, ordinary educational-institution donations reverted to a 1x deduction — the EEF-specific 2x deduction is a distinct, still-active carve-out through 2028. (Search-snippet synthesis, **[UNVERIFIED]** on the 2568 general reversion detail — worth independent legal verification before using in deck.)

## 3. Funding-Mechanism Precedents (summary, cross-referenced from above)
1. **EEF (กสศ.) itself** — 7.99B baht/year FY2567, already channels government + donor money to ~2.6M poor students; direct integration partner for คว้าฝัน's free-tier funding. [VERIFIED]
2. **2x tax-deductible donations to EEF through 2028** — ready-made corporate incentive structure AIS or university partners could use to fund คว้าฝัน seats. [VERIFIED]
3. **KFC × กสศ. "Bucket Search"** — direct precedent of a corporate brand co-funding a กสศ.-linked youth program at a few-hundred-to-1,000-student scale. [UNVERIFIED but strong analog]
4. **Central Pattana × vocational colleges** — direct precedent for corporate-sponsored career-exposure/mentoring content for junior-high students, conceptually identical to คว้าฝัน's "A Day With..." videos. [UNVERIFIED]
5. **ทุนนวัตกรรมสายอาชีพชั้นสูง (EEF vocational innovation fund)** — ~2,500 full scholarships/year (tuition + stipend) — useful as a "downstream conversion" partner: คว้าฝัน could route matched students into this exact fund. [UNVERIFIED]
